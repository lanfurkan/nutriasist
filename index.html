1 · Termal radyasyon Exam Review #5 · ASEO01

Wien yasası: tepe dalga boyuER #5

λmax = 2898 µm·KT
  • T: mutlak sıcaklık (K) → °C + 273
  • λmax: en çok yayılan dalga boyu (µm)
  • Sıcaklık ↑ → λmax ↓ (ters orantı)
300 K → 9.7 µm (LWIR) · 600 K → 4.8 µm · 1000 K → 2.9 µm (MWIR) · 5800 K (güneş) → 0.5 µm
⚠ °C'yi K'e çevirmeyi unutma: −8 °C = 265 K

Stefan–Boltzmann: toplam güçASEO01 #4

M = ε · σ · T4
  • M: radiant exitance (W/m²)
  • ε: emissivity (0–1)
  • σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W·m⁻²·K⁻⁴
T 2 katına çıkarsa M = 2⁴ = 16 katı
⚠ Planck = spektral dağılım · Wien = tepe · S-B = toplam

Radyasyon–madde etkileşimiLearning outcomes

α + ρ + τ = 1
  • α: soğurulan · ρ: yansıyan · τ: geçen
  • Kirchhoff (dengede): ε = α
  • Opak yüzey (τ = 0): ρ = 1 − ε
ε = 0.9 olan opak yüzey → ρ = 1 − 0.9 = 0.1
⚠ Düşük ε (parlak metal) → çok yansıtır → soğuk görünür

Foton enerjisiFormül sayfası

E = h·ν = h·cλ
  • h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s
  • c = 3×10⁸ m/s
  • λ ↑ → E ↓ → photon dedektörde cut-off
Pratik: E[eV] ≈ 1.24 / λ[µm] · λc ≈ 1.24 / Eg

1 · Thermal radiation Exam Review #5 · ASEO01

Wien's law: peak wavelengthER #5

λmax = 2898 µm·KT
  • T: absolute temperature (K) → °C + 273
  • λmax: most strongly emitted wavelength (µm)
  • Temperature ↑ → λmax ↓ (inverse relation)
300 K → 9.7 µm (LWIR) · 600 K → 4.8 µm · 1000 K → 2.9 µm (MWIR) · 5800 K (sun) → 0.5 µm
⚠ Don't forget to convert °C to K: −8 °C = 265 K

Stefan–Boltzmann: total powerASEO01 #4

M = ε · σ · T4
  • M: radiant exitance (W/m²)
  • ε: emissivity (0–1)
  • σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W·m⁻²·K⁻⁴
If T doubles, M = 2⁴ = 16 times
⚠ Planck = spectral distribution · Wien = peak · S-B = total

Radiation–matter interactionLearning outcomes

α + ρ + τ = 1
  • α: absorbed · ρ: reflected · τ: transmitted
  • Kirchhoff (at equilibrium): ε = α
  • Opaque surface (τ = 0): ρ = 1 − ε
Opaque surface with ε = 0.9 → ρ = 1 − 0.9 = 0.1
⚠ Low ε (shiny metal) → reflects a lot → looks cold

Photon energyFormula sheet

E = h·ν = h·cλ
  • h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s
  • c = 3×10⁸ m/s
  • λ ↑ → E ↓ → cut-off in photon detectors
Practical: E[eV] ≈ 1.24 / λ[µm] · λc ≈ 1.24 / Eg

Inverse square lawCockpit worksheet Ex.1

E = Φ4π · d²
  • E: irradiance or illuminance
  • d: distance to source (m)
  • Plotting E against 1/d² gives a straight line
20 lx @ 0.5 m → at 1 m: 20 × (0.5/1)² = 5 lx

  • f ↑ → FOV ↓ (narrow area, more detail)

IFOV ≈ pf · FOVtotal = IFOV × N

  • N: number of pixels
  • Result in radians (1 µrad = 10⁻⁶ rad)
p = 15 µm, f = 100 mm → 15×10⁻⁶ / 0.1 = 150 µrad

Ground Sample DistanceER #11

GSD = α × R
  • α: IFOV (radians)
  • R: range (m)
  • Smaller GSD = better resolution

f-numberER #13

f/# = fD
  • f: focal length · D: aperture (lens) diameter
  • D ↑ → f/# ↓ → more light
  • Collected light ∝ 1/(f/#)²
f = 100 mm, D = 25 mm → f/4 · f/2 → f/4: light ¼
⚠ It's f/D, not D/f!

Airy disk & NyquistFormula sheet

r = 1.22 λ·fD · fN = 12D

  • fN: highest frequency that can be imaged correctly
  • D (in Nyquist): pixel pitch

Thin lens equationFormula sheet

  • so: object distance
  • si: image distance
  • For a distant object si ≈ f
